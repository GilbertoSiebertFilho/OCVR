# OCVR, the evidence behind each rule: a literature check of the variable-rate economics method

*Olds College OFPE team. Literature review supporting the OCVR app (Olds College Variable-Rate economics, version 1.1.0), which evaluates on-farm seed-rate and nitrogen-rate trials by NDVI zone and answers whether variable rate would have paid against a flat rate. It was checked on three on-farm trials of 2026.*

*Prepared 29 October 2026. Citation keys are first author and year, as in Section 12 and `references.csv`; same-author, same-year works carry a letter (Harker 2017 = canola seeding rate and seed size, Harker 2017a = nitrogen and seeding rate versus novel inputs; Mangin 2022 = part I yield, Mangin 2022a = part II grain nitrogen). Every statement about a paper comes from the verified evidence extracted from it (`evidence_by_rule.json`), and every reference says how far it could be read (full text, abstract or publisher preview, not read). Appendix A gives the method of the check.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

**What was checked.** The method of the app was written out as 28 rules (zones Z1–Z4, join J1–J4, response R1–R5, economics E1–E11, treatment strips T1, prices and Prairie context P1–P2, the cleaned yield map C1), each with its parameters as coded. For each rule, every cited paper was read (the full text where it was open, otherwise the abstract or the publisher's preview) and judged on whether it says what the rule needs. Each judgement was then contested by a second, independent reader with the paper in hand, and a final critic listed the topics with no reference at all. 112 rule–paper pairs were judged: 16 support the rule as implemented, 69 support part of it, 20 do not support it, and 7 could not be read far enough to judge. Of the 28 rules, one is well supported (E11, one season is ex post), 21 are partly supported, and 6 are house conventions with no published counterpart (J4, R4, R5, E8, E10, P1). 69 further references were found and confirmed, of which 63 were adopted (the other 6 were already in the earlier base), plus 14 for topics nothing cited (the Sentinel-2 product and its cloud mask, the NDVI itself, the Prairie NDVI–yield window, within-field variability, seeding-rate responses, the cost of not cleaning). All 147 DOIs of the final list resolve on Crossref or OpenAlex with a matching title, first author and year.

**What the literature supports.** The frame of the app is the published one: the partial budget and the value of information of Swinton & Lowenberg-DeBoer (Swinton 1998; Bullock 2002); the temporal-mean zoning of Blackmore 2000 carried to satellite NDVI by Lenoir 2024 (Sentinel-2) and Basso 2019 (Landsat); on-farm precision experimentation with randomised plots, buffers at the plot ends and footprint overlay (Bullock 2019; Trevisan 2021; dos Santos 2025); a parametric response with the plateau form reported beside the quadratic (Cerrato 1990; Bullock 1994; Miguez 2022); standard errors clustered by the randomisation unit (Cameron 2015; Abadie 2023); the in-sample value of VR being biased upward by construction and the remedy of judging it on data that did not choose it (Mieno 2024; Chernozhukov 2018; Athey 2016); a minimum worthwhile gain because payoff functions are flat near the optimum (Pannell 2006; Pannell 2019); and the ex-post nature of one season's optimum (Rajsic 2008; Bullock 2020; Puntel 2016).

**What the literature does not support, and what we did about it.** The previous literature base (27 October) attributed to Mieno 2024 a zone × rate test and a cross-fitting procedure that the paper does not contain, to Bullock 2019 a grower-compensation computation it does not describe, to Bullock 2000 a sentence that is not in its abstract, and to Lee 2012, Gauci 2024, Malhi 2001 and Mangin 2022 findings they do not report; its table of defaults named a 5-fold cross-fit, 500 resamples, 300 observation units per zone, a 50 % footprint share and a 4-SD filter that the app did not have (its bootstrap default was then 100). Those attributions are corrected here and in the README and the PDF report. The check also changed the app in four places (Section 11): Wald tests are now judged against F(h, plots − 1) rather than chi-square, because the chi-square reference over-rejects with few clusters (Cameron 2015); the bootstrap default went from 100 to 500 resamples (1,000 advised for a report to publish, a house choice; Hernandez 2008 found that bootstrap intervals on the optimum "showed great potential" and Jaynes 2011 used 1,000 Monte Carlo realizations); the label "DIFM grower compensation" became "the ex-post cost of the trial to the grower" (Puntel 2024); and the advice to cut a rate now carries the warning that under-application costs more than over-application when the true response is steeper than estimated (Babcock 1992; Rajsic 2008).

**House choices.** The following have no published counterpart and are presented as calibrations, not findings: the median of the three highest-NDVI dates of a 15 June – 15 August window, the 3 × 3 smoothing, the equal-area tertiles and the 0.5 ha patch rule (Z1, Z3); the 95 % footprint share and the unit thresholds of 10 records and 150 m² (J2, J3); reading a trial design back from the as-applied map, with its separation score, shape tests and mixed-plot rule (J4); the merge rule of 20 units and 3 design rates per zone (R4); the zone × rate Wald test as one of the two conditions for VR, beside the cross-fitted gain (R5); the thresholds $3/ac, 70 % of repeats and p < 0.10 and their AND logic (E4, E5), calibrated on one synthetic control; the one-sided linear extension of a status quo outside the trial range and its 25 % cut-off (E9); the ± 30 % price grid (E7); the corner-share rule for the NDVI prescription (E10); and the realised-trial accounting with every input at its status quo (E8). Each is a defensible choice validated on the app's synthetic trials and the three worked cases, without outside validation.

**Open points.** Seven rule–paper pairs could not be judged: Grant 2012 could not be read at all; Malhi 2001 could not be read for P2 (its abstract and introduction, read for P1, show nothing on the N plateau); and Taylor 2007, Bullock 2000 (R2), Bullock 2019 (J4), Piepho 2011 and Lee 2012 were read only at abstracts that do not settle the rule. Blackmore 2000 is closed: its abstract and introduction snippets were read on the ScienceDirect preview, and its equations only as Lenoir 2024 reproduces them. Three published results run against the zone method and are stated as limits rather than resolved: Georgi 2018 discards exactly the saturated high-NDVI images the window prefers; Lenoir 2024 found the NDVI–yield correlation of Belgian winter wheat falling after the end of May and chose its image with the yield map in hand; Blackmore 2003 found multi-year trends washing out on four English fields. No published NDVI study recovers unstable zones with a temporal CV (Lenoir 2024: sensitivity zero). For the Prairies the only timing evidence located is at MODIS scale (Mkhabela 2011), and it supports the window.

## 2. How to read this document

Sections 3 to 9 go through the 28 rules in the order of the app's steps. Each rule has four parts: *what the app does* (with the parameters as coded on 29 October 2026), *what the literature says* (the specific finding, method or number in each paper that bears on the rule, with the key of the paper and how far it was read), *support* (well supported, partly supported, or house choice, with the house choices named), and *what changed after the check*. Section 10 lists the house contributions in one place, Section 11 the changes made to the app and its documents, Section 12 the verified reference list. Appendix A describes the check itself.

The support levels mean: **well supported**, every element of the rule, including its numbers, is found in a paper that was read; **partly supported**, the approach is published but one or more of the rule's parameters, thresholds or specifics are the app's own; **house choice**, no paper read describes the rule, and the citations in the earlier literature base supported at most the premise.
