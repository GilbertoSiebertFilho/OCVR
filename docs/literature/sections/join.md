## 5. Assigning yield records to plots and zones

Rules J1 to J4 take the cleaned yield map (OCCD) and the trial design and turn them into the analysis units the response models use. Every reference states how it was read (full text, abstract only, or not read); bracketed numbers are those of section B of `LITERATURE.md`.

### J1. Each yield record is an area, assigned to a plot by overlay

**What the app does.** Every cleaned yield-monitor record becomes a rectangle, swath width × distance travelled, centred on the logged point along the heading corrected to grid north (`yield_polygons`, `assign_yield`). The rectangle is overlaid on the trimmed plots and the record is assigned by the area inside a plot, not by the position of the point. A point mode (point-in-polygon) is used when the file lacks a heading, a distance or a swath; only when the swath or the distance is missing does it flag that unit areas are counts, not m².

**What the literature says.** The direct counterpart is dos Santos 2025 (full text). Its "simple" method averages the observations that fall within an experimental unit (point-in-polygon); its RITAS method builds polygons from the swath and the distance between consecutive measurements, resolves overlaps by keeping the first measurement, and tracks passes that would collect grain from two units. In 2,000 simulated trials (120 units, 6 N rates) the RMSE of the agronomic optimum was 1.5 kg N/ha with polygons against 5.1 with points, that of the economic optimum 1.0 against 3.1, and points biased the economic optimum by −7.9 kg N/ha against 0.1; on a strip trial the optima were 157.0 and 133.5 kg N/ha, on a checkerboard seed trial 118.7 and 92.8 × 10³ seeds/ha. The authors blame under-estimated yield where passes overlap and dilution between adjacent units. Caveats: the comparison bundles cleaning, assignment and block kriging, and units one swath wide cannot shed crossing polygons. Drummond 1999 (abstract only) derives each record's harvested area from the GPS trajectory, improving harvest-area estimates by 11 % over a constant full swath; Han 1997 (abstract only) finds effective cut widths of 1.18–2.24 m against a nominal 1.52 m but collapses units back to centroids, a precedent for the area representation, not for overlay. Bullock 2019 (full text) does not support the method: DIFM treats readings as points, 160 per 24.4 × 88.5 m plot, gathered into subplots of about 20 m; it only shares the recognition that a swath can straddle two plots, handled by removing the whole westernmost pass of each plot. Trevisan 2021 (full text) averages the points inside 37–87 m² polygons. Sudduth 2007 (abstract only; closed access) is a cleaning tool that deletes single records and says nothing about assignment.

**Support.** Partly: the area representation and the cost of point assignment are published (dos Santos 2025, Drummond 1999, Han 1997); the rectangle centred on the logged point, the direct overlay on plots rather than a fine grid, and the reliance on an upstream-cleaned map are house choices.

**What changed after the check.** The overlay now cites dos Santos 2025 instead of Bullock 2019 and Sudduth 2007; Bullock 2019 is kept for J2 and stated as point-based; Sudduth 2007 moves to C1; Drummond 1999 and Han 1997 are added. The section C table of `LITERATURE.md` said "drop footprints < 50 % inside a plot" against the implemented 95 % and is corrected.

### J2. Trimmed plot ends and the 95 % containment rule

**What the app does.** Each plot is shortened by 10 m at both ends of its long axis (`trim_plots`; side trim optional, default 0 m). The trim is taken off the plot polygon itself, so a wedge or trapezoid clipped to the boundary loses its full 10 m along the whole end edge; a plot shorter than 21 m keeps a 1 m strip so its records are counted and then dropped by the unit rule. A record is kept only when at least 95 % of its footprint lies inside one trimmed plot; straddling records are dropped and counted (`min_share = 0` switches to clip-and-weight).

**What the literature says.** Bullock 2019 (full text, p. 2743) is the precedent for the trim: DIFM places "approximately 10 m-long transitional buffer zones" at the ends of each plot because yield monitors read with a delay between yield zones and applicators lag at treatment boundaries; DIFM does not use buffer data; plots are 75–95 m long. Kyveryga 2018 (full text, p. 199) requires a clean pass within one treatment and removes the headlands and about the first 50 ft of each pass, a start-of-pass trim only. Alesso 2021 (full text, p. 1370) sets a minimum plot length of 36 m, about 18 s, for actuator, threshing and monitor delays, in a simulation that assigns no records. Chung 2002 (full text, Tables 3–4) measured yield delays of 12–16, 15–23 and 10–17 s in three fields, moisture delays of 14–23 s, and differences of 1–3 s between regions of one field; a wrong delay blurs region edges into a saw-tooth, so transition records stay unreliable after one correction. Lark 2003 (abstract only) accounts for the combine's smoothing of the mass-flow signal. Trevisan 2021 (full text, p. 350) is the alternative: plots long enough for at least 30 s of harvest, transition points kept. Gauci 2024 (full text, p. 774) explicitly does not investigate the minimum length over which a monitor detects a yield difference and contains no lag, delay or transition content; it reports under 3 % error on strip totals inside the calibration range and over 15 % outside. Lee 2012 (abstract only; no open copy) proposes phase-correlation delay identification, accurate in about 90 % of test sets, with no delay magnitude. No paper contains the 95 % share.

**Support.** Partly: the 10 m trim is DIFM's published buffer length and the one-treatment rule has precedents, but the 95 % footprint share is a house choice (dos Santos 2025 apportions a crossing polygon proportionally; Bullock 2019 uses subplots inside the buffered interior).

**What changed after the check.** Delay figures now cite Chung 2002, 10–23 s and varying within a field, instead of Lee 2012, kept only as a delay-identification method; Gauci 2024 is dropped. The old arithmetic "10–15 s × 1.5–2.5 m/s = 15–35 m" applied to an uncorrected delay: OCCD corrects the delay upstream, so the trim covers the applicator's rate change and the residual delay error, with Bullock 2019 and Trevisan 2021 as precedents. The code docstring's attribution of the 95 % share to DIFM is not in Bullock 2019; it is a house choice.

### J3. Analysis units: plot × zone, with floors on records and area

**What the app does.** The kept record pieces are cut by the NDVI zone and grouped into units of one plot inside one zone (`build_units`; a `plot` mode without zones exists). A unit needs at least 10 records and 150 m² (the area test is skipped when the file gives no real area per record); unit yield is the area-weighted mean of its pieces; the applied rate is the median of the as-applied points inside it (at least 5 points, otherwise the plot median), or the design rate when there is no map; a piece on no zone takes the zone of the nearest valid cell among its 8 neighbours, and is dropped and counted when there is none. The plot, the unit of randomisation, is the cluster for every standard error downstream.

**What the literature says.** Bullock 2019 (full text) takes data from the middle 50 % of each plot and partitions the interior into three or four subplots of about 20 m (19 m in its Fig. 7), the observational unit for yield, moisture, as-applied and as-planted rates and soil type; it then tests whether SSURGO zones respond differently and classifies areas by GWR. It describes the aggregation only as a "resampling process" and never names the statistic; per-subplot counts sometimes quoted for it are arithmetic on its example, not printed values. Trevisan 2021 (full text) subdivides each plot into 10–20 polygons of 37–87 m², uses the average of the points in each (2,816–3,276 units per field) and the recorded rate after a 3 × IQR filter. Cho 2021 (full text) runs a single strip (9 m in grain fields, 24 m in dairy fields) across at least two yield-stability zones and puts the zone into the model (`Yield ~ Zone + Nitrogen:Zone + …`) on 553–5,220 raw points per field; in one field the N response was +0.25 Mg/ha in one zone and −0.25 in the other. Marchant 2019 (full text) adds zone × treatment interactions to a mixed model on 4,524 cleaned points and gives the only sufficiency figures found, 100 observations for a variogram and 10 rows of the standard treatment, both whole-experiment requirements. Alesso 2021 (full text) is a simulation on 81 m² pixels with 324–1,296 m² plots and supports only that units of tens to hundreds of m² work. Gauci 2024 (full text) states that its result does not characterise what a monitor can discern within a plot.

**Support.** Partly: sub-plot observational units and zone-specific responses are published practice, but the plot × zone intersection, the 10-record and 150 m² floors, the median applied rate, the area weighting and the design-rate fall-back are house choices (published units are 37–87 m² in Trevisan 2021 and about 20 m × header width in Bullock 2019; Trevisan 2021 uses the mean and Bullock 2019 names no statistic).

**What changed after the check.** Gauci 2024 is dropped; the claim that DIFM "averages" its subplots is withdrawn, since Bullock 2019 names no statistic; Trevisan 2021, Cho 2021 and Marchant 2019 are added; the unit thresholds and the median are stated as house choices. No contradiction with any paper was found; the gaps are absences.

### J4. Design levels of a second input read from its as-applied map

**What the app does.** When a second input has no design file, each plot takes the median of the as-applied points inside its trimmed interior (at least 5 points; `rates_from_asapplied`). For k = 2 … 15 the sorted plot medians are split by an exact one-dimensional partition minimising the within-group sum of squares by dynamic programming (`_partition_1d`), and each split is scored by separation: the smallest gap between adjacent groups divided by the largest spacing inside any group (`rate_levels`). The best k must score at least 2 with no rival within 0.85 of it, its gap must reach a noise floor of max(6 × the precision of a plot median, 2 % of the median), and two shape tests catch levels read as one (`_merged_levels`): an internal jump over 6 × a group's other spacings, or a group wider (p10–p90) than 2.5 × the tightest level. Under 24 plots the required score is 4 at 8 plots, falling to 2.5 at 23, and the design is flagged; otherwise the app refuses and asks for a design file. Plots whose p10 and p90 sit nearest two different levels, or whose spread exceeds half the local step, and in either case 3 × the typical plot spread, are mixed and get no design rate (`_mixed_plots`). On the sweeps of `tests/test_levels.py` no wrong number of levels was returned on designs of 20 or more plots per level, refusing about 1 clean map in 150; 5-plot-per-level designs were read in 90–94 %; a gradient on 8–20 plots slipped through in 2–4 % of cases, always with the small-design warning.

**What the literature says.** No published method reconstructs a trial design from applicator logs. Trevisan 2021 (full text, p. 350) names the practice, grouping as-applied rates into a few levels "historically" for categorical ANOVA, gives no algorithm and keeps the rate continuous; it pre-filters as-applied points at 3 × IQR (p. 345). Hegedus 2023 (full text, p. 680) uses the applicator's as-applied N as a continuous predictor and never mentions a design file. Colaço 2014 (abstract only) classes 30.6 % of logged points as application errors (10 % off target), about 70 % of them at rate changes or during acceleration, the physical basis of the mixed-plot rule; Fulton 2013 (abstract only) finds applicators within 10 % of target less than 45 % of the time, so the prescription cannot stand for the delivered rate. Fisher 1958 (abstract only) is the minimum-within-group-variance grouping the partition implements and Wang 2011 (full text) its dynamic-programming solution with an optimality guarantee; neither covers the separation score. Bullock 2019 could not be read for this check (abstract only; its full text, read for J1–J3, reports nothing on recovering levels), so it is not cited for J4.

**Support.** House choice: the premise (read the applied rate, not the prescription) and the partition are published; the separation score and its threshold of 2, the ambiguity and shape tests, the small-design rule, the p10/p90 mixed-plot rule and the plot median are unreferenced calibrations validated only on the sweeps above.

**What changed after the check.** J4 is recorded as a house contribution with no published counterpart; Fisher 1958 and Wang 2011 are cited for the partition only, since the app does use an exact 1-D partition; Fulton 2013 is cited for the premise only, not for plot medians; Colaço 2014, Fulton 2013 and Trevisan 2021 cover as-applied error and filtering, nothing more.
